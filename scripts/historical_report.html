What each row can tell us

A snapshot contains one row per reported vehicle entity. Each row can carry a trip ID, route ID, vehicle ID, location, feed timestamp and a vehicle timestamp. The service date and scheduled start time help distinguish instances of the same trip, including services whose GTFS clock passes 24:00.

Example journeys near Dublin

These traces show what reconstruction looks like before identifying routes. Select a trip to see its reported path and position freshness filters.

Examples come from the morning of 13 October 2025. They group points by route, trip, vehicle, service date and start time, and keep one point per vehicle timestamp. The examples favour routes with many distinct coordinates. They use reports no more than two minutes old and split at gaps over three minutes. Lines connect GPS reports and can cut across corners. The four examples are not a representative sample.

Analyses we could build

  1. Journey time bands by section

    Once we identify a route, infer when each trip crosses two fixed points. Plot median and 90th percentile journey times by direction and departure hour. Show the number of usable trips and reject crossings that fall inside long GPS gaps.

  2. Service gaps and bunching

    At a station or other point on a matched route, plot the time between consecutive observed vehicles. A day-by-hour heatmap would show where gaps cluster. Use collector coverage and position freshness to flag intervals where a missing vehicle could inflate the gap.

  3. Repeated slow sections

    Match trajectories to the track and compare time spent on each section across trips. A distance-by-time plot can reveal repeated slow approaches or unusually long station stops. Compare like directions and stop patterns, and avoid treating missing GPS points as stopped trains.

  4. Replay an unusual day

    Overlay movement diagrams for a day with unusually long gaps or journeys and a comparable baseline day. Use the sequence of observed movements to describe how service recovered. Explaining the cause would require independent incident information.

  5. Scheduled versus observed service

    With an October 2025 timetable covering the relevant days, join historical trip IDs and calendars, then compare estimated crossings with scheduled stop times. Plot delay distributions and unmatched trips separately. Cancellation estimates would also need evidence that the feed captured running trains reliably.

  6. How reliable was the source feed?

    Plot stale and repeated positions by route and time. Compare the fraction of trips we can reconstruct under different freshness thresholds. This shows where the archive is suitable for service analysis before we draw conclusions about operations.

Source: archived NTA GTFS Realtime vehicle positions in irish-rail-data-prod/gtfs_rt/vehicle_positions/. The 2026 timetable in this project applies to a later period.
